# Defense Readiness Rubric

An honest, calibrated **0–100 readiness score** plus per-dimension diagnosis. Adapted from peer-review quality rubrics, but re-pointed at *surviving a defense*, not *passing journal review*. The goal is honest triage, not false comfort.

> Use it twice: once after the weakness audit (baseline exposure), and again after answers/mock are prepared (improvement). Show the delta — it tells the student whether prep is actually working.

---

## IRON RULES

- **IRON RULE R1 — Score, then justify.** Assign each dimension's number first, then write the one-line reason. (Prevents being nice in words and harsh in numbers, or vice-versa.)
- **IRON RULE R2 — Evidence-anchored.** Every dimension score cites Weakness Ledger IDs and/or specific sections. No vibes.
- **IRON RULE R3 — Honest over kind.** If exposure is high, say so. A student who hears "62, major-revision-tier, here are your 3 CRITICAL exposures" prepares better than one told "looks good."

---

## The six dimensions (weighted)

| # | Dimension | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Contribution clarity** | Can the delta be stated in one sentence and defended as thesis-worthy? (Lens 3) | 20% |
| D2 | **Evidence–claim fit** | Do claims stay within what the evidence supports? Overclaim density. (Lens 1, A1) | 25% |
| D3 | **Method / argument rigor** | Sound *by the paradigm's standards* (see `discipline-profiles.md`): baselines/stats (quant), triangulation/positionality (qual), proof validity (formal), source criticism (interpretive), doctrinal accuracy (legal), craft/concept link (creative) | 20% |
| D4 | **Limitation honesty** | Are limitations/scope acknowledged and bounded, or hidden? | 10% |
| D5 | **Presentation** | Slides/exegesis/oral as applicable: readable, consistent with the thesis, no overclaim. *If the defense has no slides (common in humanities/law vivas), drop D5 and redistribute its weight.* | 10% |
| D6 | **Q&A robustness** | Quality of prepared answers + mock-defense performance on the Top-10 | 15% |

`overall = Σ(dimension_score × weight)`, rounded.

> Weights above are the **empirical-quant default**. Shift them to the paradigm: interpretive/normative work weights D3 (source/doctrinal rigor) and D1 (contribution) higher and may set D5 = 0; creative work weights the artifact–exegesis link. Pull the emphasis from `discipline-profiles.md`.

## Per-dimension descriptors (anchor the 0–100)

Score each dimension on this 5-band scale, then pick a number in the band.

| Band | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | 85–100 | Defensible as-is; an examiner would struggle to land a hit |
| Solid | 70–84 | Minor exposure; clean bounded answers exist |
| Shaky | 55–69 | Real exposure; needs prepared concessions and rehearsal |
| Weak | 40–54 | A prepared examiner can corner the student here |
| Critical | 0–39 | Foundation-level problem; high risk of a damaging exchange |

Examples (across paradigms):
- **D2 = 45 (Weak, empirical-quant):** "Central claim of real-world effectiveness rests on simulation only (W1, §4); no field data. Overclaim verbs throughout §5."
- **D3 = 48 (Weak, interpretive):** "Central reading rests on three passages; the archive holds counter-evidence (W2) the thesis never addresses."
- **D1 = 78 (Solid):** "Contribution stated cleanly, but its generality/originality is slightly oversold (W4)."

## Overall readiness tiers (decision mapping)

| Score | Tier | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| **≥ 80** | **Defense-ready** | Polish the Top-10 answers; rehearse timing. |
| **65–79** | **Minor exposure** | A few bounded concessions to prepare; drill the 2–3 shaky questions. |
| **50–64** | **Major exposure** | Several CRITICAL/MAJOR weaknesses need concede-and-redirect scripts; serious mock-defense reps before the date. |
| **< 50** | **High risk** | Foundation-level issues. Prioritize the single most dangerous flaw; consider whether materials/slides need changes before the defense. |

This mirrors review decision tiers but is framed as *exposure*, not *accept/reject*. Even a "<50" thesis can be defended with the right concession strategy — the tier tells the student **where the danger is and how hard to prepare**, not that they will fail.

---

## Anchor caps (mechanical; make the score reproducible)

Model-scored rubrics drift between runs. These caps are absolute and override any per-dimension generosity. "Unmitigated" = no `W#-A#` downgrade ruling and no material revision.

- ≥1 unmitigated CRITICAL → **overall ≤ 74** (never "Defense-ready").
- ≥2 unmitigated CRITICAL → **overall ≤ 64**.
- Each unmitigated CRITICAL → **D2 ≤ 55**.
- No mock defense run yet → **D6 ≤ 65**, labeled "unrehearsed".
- The **delta** may move only on: (a) revised materials, (b) mock-defense results, (c) ledger amendments. Drafting nicer answers does **not** move D1–D5 — preparation quality shows up through D6, not through grade inflation.

## Self-check
1. Did I score before justifying (R1)?
2. Does each dimension cite a ledger ID or section (R2)?
3. Is the overall tier consistent with the number of unmitigated CRITICAL weaknesses? (Several CRITICALs should not yield "Defense-ready".)
4. Did I frame the tier as *where to prepare*, not as a verdict on the student?
5. Do all anchor caps hold? (Never ship a cap-violating score.)

> Hand-off: the score + Top-3 exposures headline the day-of one-pager; the per-dimension lines feed the "weakness radar & calibration" tier of the output.
