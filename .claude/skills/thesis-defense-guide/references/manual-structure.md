# Defense Stage Strategy

| Defense stage | Main committee concern | Question emphasis | Safest answer posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal defense | Is the topic valuable and feasible? | Research gap, route choice, expected validation, risk control | Show feasibility without claiming finished results |
| Midterm defense | Is the project on track? | Completed work, unfinished work, timeline risk, method corrections | Be concrete about progress and remaining risks |
| Pre-defense | Is the thesis ready to submit? | Structure, evidence gaps, contribution wording, missing comparisons | Prioritize revisions and tighten claims |
| Final defense / viva | Are the contribution and evidence defensible? | Novelty, rigor, limitations, reproducibility, generalization | Defend bounded claims and concede real limits |
| Written committee review | Is the written argument traceable? | Source evidence, compliance, argument structure, reviewer objections | Make evidence and revisions easy to audit |

## Red-Line Phrases To Avoid

Replace generic red lines with thesis-specific versions whenever possible (the method + a thesis-specific table live in `answer-coaching-framework.md`). Examples span paradigms — use the ones that fit the work:

- "This definitely works / holds in all cases."
- "This proves my conclusion." (when the evidence only supports it within a boundary)
- "This limitation does not affect the conclusion at all."
- "Future work will solve this." as a substitute for answering the current limitation.
- *Empirical:* "This is already validated in real scenarios." when only simulation or offline tests exist; "The method is optimal." when only a heuristic or empirical best case is shown.
- *Interpretive / historical:* "The sources clearly show…" when the reading is contested or the archive is mixed.
- *Legal / normative:* "This is settled / obviously right." when the authority or premise is contestable.
